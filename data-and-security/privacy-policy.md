### Cookies, tracking technologies and Do Not Track

#### Cookies

A cookie is a small file a website stores in your browser. Sortment uses cookies and similar technologies to keep the website working, to measure how it is used, and to understand which businesses visit.

When you first visit sortment.com we ask for your consent. If you choose Reject, Google tags run in a limited, cookieless mode and Microsoft Clarity and rb2b do not load. Our company-identification tools (lemlist and Happierleads) still run, as described below. If you choose Accept, we also enable the analytics and visitor-identification tools described below. You can change your choice at any time by clearing your browser's site data for sortment.com, which shows the consent banner again.

Some pages embed content from other services, such as YouTube videos or Calendly booking pages. Those services may set their own cookies, which are governed by their own privacy policies.

#### Tracking technologies

On sortment.com we use:

• Google Tag Manager, Google Analytics 4 and Google Ads, to measure visits, page views and actions such as booking a call. Until you accept cookies, these run in Google's consent mode without setting analytics or advertising cookies.

• Microsoft Clarity, to understand how visitors use pages (for example scrolling and clicks). Loaded only after you accept cookies.

• rb2b, to identify business visitors to our website, which may include matching a visit to a person's professional profile (such as name, job title, company and LinkedIn profile) so our team can follow up. Loaded only after you accept cookies. rb2b identifies visitors in the United States only.

• lemlist and Happierleads, to identify the companies that visit our website so our sales team can follow up. These run on every visit, whatever you choose in the cookie banner, and identify companies rather than individual people.

For logged-in users to Sortment Cloud deployments, we also use third-party tracking technologies (Sentry, Cohere) to capture usage data to detect, prevent and address technical issues and provide support.

If you have been identified through one of these tools and do not want to be contacted, or want us to delete that information, email us using the details in the ‘Contacting Sortment About Your Privacy’ section of this Privacy Policy.

#### Do Not Track

"Do Not Track" is a browser setting that asks websites not to track you. There is no common standard for responding to it, so sortment.com does not change its behaviour based on it. Instead, you can control tracking on our site through the cookie consent banner, as described above.
